# Concept Maps: Navigating the 21 Modules of the Course

Opening the course reveals 21 modules covering tracking, metrics, product, marketing, data warehousing, streaming, advanced SQL, statistics, dbt, philosophy of causality, AI for data work, and agentic AI. This breadth can easily overwhelm learners, leading to two common mistakes: passively studying everything in order without understanding why, or jumping between topics chasing what seems urgent. Both waste energy and hinder building solid skills.

## Real-World Problem

A student aiming to improve data work faces dashboards, SQL, models, pipelines, and AI tools. Without a conceptual map, they risk accumulating disconnected knowledge, unable to link phases and outputs. The key question is not "Can I recite the theory?", but: **Which decision improves if I apply this lesson well?** Without guidance, costly mistakes happen - launching features too early, reallocating budgets without causal basis, fragile data models, or elegant but impractical AI workflows.

## Conceptual Model

Use this three-step framework to govern your study:

| Level | Guiding Question | Expected Output |
|---|---|---|
| Phenomenon | What is really happening? | Scope, segments, and assumptions |
| Evidence | What data would change our view? | Metrics, observations, and guardrails |
| Decision | What will we do if the signal holds? | Action, owner, timing, and stop criteria |

This model prevents falling into pure theory or unreflective operations.

## Rigorous Formalization

Formalize each lesson as a decision chain:

| Element | Operational Definition | Quality Control |
|---|---|---|
| Input | Notes, concept maps, checklists, exercises, projects, feedback | Declared source, period, granularity, completeness |
| Hypothesis | Expected relation between study behavior, output, and skill | Assumptions documented before analysis |
| Metric | Continuity, completed exercises, artifact quality, ability to explain choices | Explicit denominator, segment, time window |
| Guardrail | What must not degrade during optimization | Understanding, rigor, sustainability, autonomy |
| Decision | What to study now, what to postpone, output to produce | Owner, deadline, review criteria |

Effective formalization lets others reconstruct and challenge reasoning without ambiguity.

## Example or Case Study

Imagine a weekly review with student, mentor, and portfolio reviewer deciding whether to move to advanced modules M20 and M21 (AI and agentic AI). The student knows SQL, metrics, and dashboards but hasn't produced a decision memo with leakage and drift checks.

| Observation | Prudent Interpretation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Student wants to use AI on a real case | Valid motivation but lacks rigor | Verify foundations in M3, M14, M17, and M19 |
| Has dataset and business question | Positive operational signal | Prepare baseline and guardrail sheet |
| Can't define owner and stop criteria | Premature automation | Postpone agentic AI; work on decision memo first |

The goal is not a perfect answer but a defensible study decision.

## Lab / Exercise

| Level | Activity | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | Summarize your professional goal and pick 5 priority modules | Mini learning brief |
| Intermediate | Design an 8-week sequence with practice, reviews, and outputs | Study plan with deliverables |
| Research-grade | Write a memo justifying when to introduce M20-M21 in your path | Revisable decision memo |

**Recommended datasets and materials:** study calendar, current portfolio, notes from completed lessons, real dashboard or analysis, AI use case, list of risks to control.

## Common Pitfall to Avoid

The most frequent mistake is treating "Concept Maps" as a one-time read and then forgetting it. Instead, revisit the map whenever you change goals, lose continuity, or want to jump to advanced tools.

A practical fix: before starting a new module, write three lines: the skill you want to build, the output you'll produce, and the previous module you must be able to explain. Missing any of these means you're probably studying out of curiosity, not building transferable skills.

## Quiz or Checkpoint

1. How many phases does the course path have?
2. Which phase connects roles, causality, AI-assisted data work, and agentic AI?
3. Why do M20 and M21 come after modules on metrics, SQL, statistics, analytics engineering, and causality?
4. What output would you use to prove you truly mastered a phase?
5. What risk do you run if you use AI or agentic AI before having clear data and owners?

If you can't answer specifically, return to the real-world problem before continuing.

## Conclusion

This map does not simplify the course artificially; it makes it governable. Following the phased progression, the path remains complete yet readable, advanced yet concrete, technical yet oriented to real work.

You have truly absorbed the map when you can use it to explain the path without jargon, apply it to your professional goal, and defend a study sequence with outputs, limits, and next checks.
