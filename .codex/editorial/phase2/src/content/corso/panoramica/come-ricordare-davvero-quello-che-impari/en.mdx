# Reproducibility Mindset: Prioritize Rigor Over Speed

Have you ever delivered an analysis that was crucial for a decision, only to find yourself unable to reproduce it a few days later? This is not a memory issue but a reproducibility problem. In data work, speed without traceability becomes technical debt: what seems efficient today turns into debugging, mistrust, and fragile decisions tomorrow.

## Real-World Problem

In 2010, economists Reinhart and Rogoff published an influential study linking public debt to economic growth, shaping fiscal policies worldwide. Three years later, a graduate student uncovered basic errors in their Excel sheet: five countries were omitted, and there was a weighting mistake. Correcting these errors significantly weakened the study's conclusions.

This issue extends beyond economics. Every day, many companies face unreproducible analyses causing confusion and wasted time. Ambiguous file names, unversioned queries, undocumented filters - all make it impossible to rerun an analysis without asking for clarifications.

## Conceptual Model

The solution is the **reproducibility mindset**: build every analytical output as if someone else must reproduce it tomorrow morning without direct contact. This relies on three essential pillars:

- **Traceability**: every step, from data source to query, must be reconstructible without asking.
- **Standards**: shared, consistent definitions to avoid misunderstandings.
- **Reviewability**: openness to peer review and maintaining a changelog.

The equation is simple: same question + same data + same logic = same result. If this fails, the method is broken.

## Rigorous Formalization

| Pillar       | Daily Minimum Practice                                                             | Why It Works                                         |
|--------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| Traceability | Save queries with date and purpose in the name, comments on source and filters     | Makes work reusable even after months               |
| Standards    | Maintain a shared metric dictionary with operational definitions and updates       | Eliminates debates over metric meanings             |
| Reviewability| Brief peer review before delivery, changelog with changes and reasons              | Catches errors before they become wrong decisions   |

Applying these practices also encourages simplifying overly complex workflows.

## Example or Case Study

**Case 1: Retention Discrepancy**
In a SaaS company, two analysts calculated retention differently because one excluded expired trials and the other did not, without documentation. After introducing a shared metric dictionary and versioned queries, the discrepancy vanished, and discussions refocused on business questions.

**Case 2: Netflix and the Experiment Board**
Netflix developed a tool to document every experiment with hypotheses, data, methods, and lessons learned. This approach transformed decisions from opinions into evidence-based discussions, fostering innovation and transparency.

## Lab / Exercise

**Basic Level**: Document a recent analysis with data source, extraction date, query, metric definition, filters, and final result. Note any traceability gaps. Estimated time: 20 minutes.

**Intermediate Level**: Have a colleague reproduce the analysis using only your documentation, without assistance. Every question they ask reveals a documentation gap. Estimated time: 1 hour.

**Research-Grade Level**: Design a reproducibility checklist for your team and measure the reproducible output rate per sprint, aiming to improve from 30-40% to 70-80% within three sprints.

**Recommended Datasets and Materials**: analysis log template, shared metric dictionary, review checklist, DVC for datasets and ML models.

## Common Mistake to Avoid

The most frequent mistake is postponing documentation due to lack of time or assuming it's unnecessary for internal use. "I'll document it later" often means "never." Every minute saved today results in hours wasted tomorrow by you or your colleagues.

## Operational Summary

Reproducibility is an investment with guaranteed returns: every minute spent making work traceable, standardized, and reviewable multiplies future efficiency. Applying the three pillars and the thirty-minute rule turns fragile analyses into solid decisions. Only transparency guards against error, and only rigor makes speed sustainable.
