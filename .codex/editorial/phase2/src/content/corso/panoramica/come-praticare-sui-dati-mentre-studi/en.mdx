# From Business Question to Analytical Question

Requests like "Can you show me how the product is doing?" are common, but jumping straight to a dashboard misses half the value of analysis. "How is it doing?" is not a question; it's a bundle of anxiety, priorities, and hidden assumptions.

## Real Problem

The issue is not lack of data but asking questions that data cannot turn into actionable decisions. A vague question leads to charts without direction. A professional analytical question reduces ambiguity by framing uncertainty into testable hypotheses, explicit comparisons, decision thresholds, and residual risk assessment.

## Conceptual Model

The translation from business question to analytical question follows four strict steps:

| Step | Clarify | Quality Indicator |
|---|---|---|
| Business | Which decision needs improvement? | The question has a decision owner |
| Analytical | What hypothesis is testable? | There is an explicit comparison |
| Measurement | Which metric and segment? | Denominator and time window are clear |
| Threshold | When do we act? | The expected result triggers a decision |

This chain is not just technical but a discipline that prevents wasting time and resources chasing useless dashboards.

## Rigorous Formalization

A complete analytical question is formulated as:

> "We need to decide whether to do X. We test if group A differs from group B on metric M, within segment S, during window W. We act if M exceeds or falls below threshold T, controlling for confounders C."

This formalization guards against shifting the question after seeing the data, keeping analysis focused and actionable.

## Example or Case Study

ShopDirect, a mid-size e-commerce, faced an 18% revenue drop. The vague question was "Why are we selling less?" Decomposition analysis broke down the problem into traffic, conversion, and average order value. Traffic and order value were stable, but conversion plummeted, mainly on iOS mobile checkout due to a bug.

The decision changed completely: instead of increasing ad spend, they fixed the bug. This case shows how a well-formulated analytical question avoids huge costs and wrong decisions.

## Lab / Exercise

### Basic Level

Turn a vague question into a specific analytical question: define metric, comparison, segment, and time window.

### Intermediate Level

Set a decision threshold before analyzing data. Write what you will do if the threshold is met or not.

### Research-Grade Level

Prepare a brief for a stakeholder including business question, analytical question, required data, possible confounders, threshold, and expected recommendation.

### Recommended Datasets and Materials

Use funnel, sales, retention, or traffic data. If no real data is available, create a synthetic CSV with channel, device, conversion, and revenue.

## Common Pitfall to Avoid

Starting from the tool instead of the question is the costliest mistake. SQL, Python, dashboards do not solve poorly formulated questions. First make the question measurable, then choose the tool.

## Operational Summary

The discipline of translating business questions into analytical ones requires practice and rigor:

- Embrace the business question without judgment.
- Formulate a testable hypothesis with subject, phenomenon, and comparison.
- Define metric, segment, and time window with surgical precision.
- Set a decision threshold before viewing data.
- Know the framework's limits and adapt when necessary.
- Use the four-line template as a contract with stakeholders.

Next time someone asks "How's it going?", respond with a question: "What decision are we trying to make?" That's where professional analysis begins.
