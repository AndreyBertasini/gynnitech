# Prioritizing Features with Data

Product roadmaps often list many potential features, but engineering capacity is limited. Prioritizing features with data helps select which to build by evaluating impact, evidence, cost, risk, and strategy, avoiding decisions based on opinions alone.

## Real-world problem

In product analytics, the backlog can become a political arena where strong voices or pressures dominate:

- The HiPPO: highest paid person's opinion
- The loudest customer threatening churn
- Recency bias making the latest issue seem most urgent
- Engineering preferences for technically interesting work
- Sales pressure to deliver promised features

Data does not eliminate politics but raises the cost of pushing features: proposers must state hypotheses, target segments, metrics, risks, and trade-offs explicitly.

## Conceptual model

A feature is a bet on future user behavior, not just a deliverable. A rigorous proposal states:

> By building **X** for segment **Y**, we expect to increase metric **Z** by **N%**, based on evidence **E**. We will verify this within **T days** by measuring **M**.

This shifts discussion from "like/dislike" to evaluating plausibility, relevance, metric choice, and cost.

## Rigorous formalization

To analyze feature prioritization with data, define:

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| Unit of analysis | user, cohort, product event, feature, or journey |
| Primary signal | activation, retention, frequency, conversion, churn, cohort value |
| Baseline | prior period, comparable group, benchmark, counterfactual scenario |
| Expected decision | product diagnosis, experiment, prioritization, UX intervention |
| Risk | mistaking available numbers for sufficient proof |

Formalization is solid if another analyst can reproduce the logic and reach the same decision.

## Example or case study

A SaaS product with 50,000 monthly active users evaluates three features:

- Onboarding templates for new users
- Advanced PDF export requested by enterprise clients
- Semantic search to improve content discovery

Using the RICE framework (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort), scores are:

`RICE Score = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort`

| Feature             | Reach | Impact | Confidence | Effort | RICE Score |
|---------------------|-------|--------|------------|--------|------------|
| Onboarding templates| 12,000| 2.0    | 0.85       | 2.0    | 10,200     |
| Advanced PDF export  | 1,800 | 1.5    | 0.90       | 1.5    | 1,620      |
| Semantic search      | 30,000| 1.0    | 0.45       | 5.0    | 2,700      |

Despite semantic search having the highest reach, its high effort and low confidence lower its priority. Onboarding wins by targeting a critical moment: first user experience.

## Lab / exercise

### Basic level

Write a one-page feature brief: decision supported, primary metric, baseline, main risk, and action if signal confirms.

### Intermediate level

Build a table with three segments or scenarios, indicating what changes, plausible alternative explanations, and controls before recommending action.

### Research-grade level

Prepare a decision memo: hypotheses, required data, exclusion criteria, quality controls, decision threshold, residual risk, and post-decision monitoring plan.

### Recommended datasets and materials

Use product events, funnels, sessions, surveys, CRM, support tickets, and experiments. If unavailable, create a synthetic dataset with at least 200 rows, temporal dimension, segments, and outcome metric.

## Common mistake to avoid

Treating data-driven prioritization as a label rather than a process: showing charts without decisions, metrics without baselines, or conclusions without stating assumptions that could invalidate them. The control question is: if this result were unstable, what wrong choice would I make?

## Quiz or checkpoint

1. What concrete decision does this lesson improve?
2. What unit of analysis makes the problem measurable?
3. What baseline avoids naive interpretation?
4. What common mistake could change the conclusion?
5. What output would you deliver to a non-technical stakeholder?

## Operational summary

Data-driven feature prioritization turns the backlog into a portfolio of bets. RICE helps compare impact, reach, confidence, and effort. The Kano Model guards against underestimating basic needs and overvaluing delighters. Cost of Delay adds the time dimension. The real leap happens post-launch by comparing estimates to outcomes: a mature team learns systematically from mistakes.

## Complementary frameworks in depth

### Kano Model

Features do not raise satisfaction equally:

- Basic needs: essential, absence causes dissatisfaction
- Performance needs: satisfaction proportional to quality
- Delighters: unexpected, generate enthusiasm but fade over time

Measuring Kano with data involves combining surveys and behavioral data, e.g., correlating feature absence with churn or usage with retention.

### Cost of Delay and WSJF

Time can be more critical than RICE score. Cost of Delay measures value lost per week of delay. WSJF (Weighted Shortest Job First) is:

`WSJF = Cost of Delay / Job Size`

Where Cost of Delay sums business value, time criticality, and risk reduction.

### Real case: Intercom

Intercom manages initiatives as "product bets" with clear theses, metrics, and verification moments, combining problem severity, segment size, strategic alignment, confidence from qualitative research and usage data, and engineering effort. This approach goes beyond numbers and requires a decision-making culture.
