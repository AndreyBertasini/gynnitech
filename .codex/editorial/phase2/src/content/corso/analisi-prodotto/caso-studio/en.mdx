# Case Study: Complete Product Analysis

Growth in acquisition does not guarantee improvements in activation, retention, or satisfaction. This case study demonstrates how to connect diverse signals  -  user journey, cohorts, friction points, hypotheses, and recommendations  -  into a coherent path toward actionable decisions.

## Real-world problem

FitTrack has experienced rapid growth over 18 months, but the teams are conflicted: marketing celebrates record downloads while product worries about hidden churn. The core challenge is to determine if this growth is sustainable or merely filling a leaky bucket.

Goal: identify which decision to improve, which data to observe, and which risks to avoid to turn analysis into action.

## Conceptual model

A straightforward yet powerful framework:

| Phase | What to clarify | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Which real decision must improve? | Decision to be made |
| Measurement | Which metric reflects the problem? | Observable data |
| Control | Which baseline makes the data interpretable? | Credible comparison |
| Action | What changes after analysis? | Next operational step |

Every technical detail should strengthen at least one of these points.

## Rigorous formalization

For FitTrack, define:

- Unit of analysis: user and signup cohort
- Signals: activation, retention, engagement, monetization
- Baseline: historical trends and segment comparisons
- Decision: product diagnosis and intervention roadmap
- Risk: mistaking volume for real value

This formalization allows reproducibility and critical evaluation.

## Example or case study

### Phase 1: Health dashboard

MAU grows but DAU/MAU ratio declines from 22% to 19%, indicating daily habit formation lags behind acquisition.

### Phase 2: Retention and cohorts

Retention worsens in newer cohorts; paid campaigns bring more users but less engaged. Growth hides declining quality.

### Phase 3: Activation and aha moment

Only 38% complete their first workout, with a median delay of 47 minutes. Activated users retain well; non-activated churn quickly. Activation is the bottleneck.

### Phase 4: Behavioral segmentation

Segments identified: inactive, curious_dormant, casual_mover, consistent_builder, athlete_power. Athlete_power users are 9% but generate most value.

### Phase 5: Driver analysis

Key behaviors predicting transition to consistent_builder:

1. Completing first workout within 15 minutes
2. Viewing progress summary immediately
3. Enabling personalized reminders
4. Joining a challenge within 7 days

### Phase 6: Operational recommendations

Three initiatives:

- Quick win: simplify onboarding to reduce time to first workout below 15 minutes
- Strategic: social challenges to convert casual_movers into consistent_builders
- Moonshot: adaptive coach to reduce churn and personalize experience

## Lab / exercise

### Basic level

Write a one-page summary of decision, metric, baseline, risk, and action for FitTrack.

### Intermediate level

Build a table with segments or periods, alternative explanations, and controls for each observed change.

### Research-grade level

Prepare a decision memo with hypotheses, data needs, exclusion criteria, quality controls, decision thresholds, risks, and monitoring plan.

### Recommended datasets and materials

Use product events, funnels, surveys, CRM, support tickets, and experiments. If real data is unavailable, create a synthetic dataset with at least 200 rows.

## Common mistake to avoid

Do not treat this case as a mere label or isolated chart. Without a clear decision, baseline, and explicit assumptions, the analysis lacks value.

## Quiz or checkpoint

1. Which concrete decision should this analysis improve?
2. Which unit of analysis makes the problem measurable?
3. Which baseline avoids naive interpretations?
4. Which common error could invalidate the conclusion?
5. How would you summarize the output for a non-technical stakeholder?

## Operational summary

A complete product analysis goes beyond numbers and charts. It delivers a clear diagnosis, identifies likely causes, and translates findings into a roadmap with measurable metrics. For FitTrack, the priority is to reduce time to first workout and convert casual users into consistent ones through social mechanisms and motivating feedback.
