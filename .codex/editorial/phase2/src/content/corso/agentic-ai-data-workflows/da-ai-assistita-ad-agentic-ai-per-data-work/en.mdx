# From Assisted AI to Agentic AI for Data Work

Moving from assisted AI to agentic AI in data work tackles a concrete challenge: how to leverage agentic AI without turning analysis into a chain of unverifiable answers. The goal is not to ask AI to "do analysis" but to design a workflow where questions, data, checks, and decisions remain transparent and traceable.

This lesson opens the module on agentic AI applied to data analysis, data engineering, and AutoML, connecting previous modules to a new way of working. The aim is to increase speed and coverage of data work while maintaining full ownership over definitions, thresholds, segments, and recommendations.

## Real-world problem

Imagine a weekly review where marketing, product, and data teams must decide what to fix first  -  funnel, tracking, campaign, model, or pipeline. Without agentic AI, analysts risk spending hours on repetitive steps; with poorly used agentic AI, they risk delivering convincing but fragile explanations. The operational problem is deciding when assisted AI suffices and when a governed agentic workflow is needed, one that makes data, assumptions, and minimal checks explicit.

## Conceptual model

The model consists of four layers:

| Level     | What to clarify                               | Example in data work                        |
|-----------|-----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Task      | Which part of the work is accelerated by AI?  | Query draft, data profile, AutoML checklist, memo |
| Context   | What information makes the output useful?     | Schema, metric, period, segment, policy    |
| Check     | How to detect if the output is wrong?         | Grain test, baseline, leakage, owner review |
| Decision  | What action follows if the check passes?      | Pipeline fix, experiment, deploy, recommendation |

This model avoids two opposite mistakes: using AI only as text autocomplete or giving it autonomy on decisions requiring business judgment. Professional skill lies in drawing this boundary.

## Rigorous formalization

To analyze the shift from assisted AI to agentic AI, define the unit of work (task, tool call, state, workflow step, or automatable decision) and link observable signals (useful autonomy, human interventions, blocked errors, cycle time, traceability). The expected decision is when assisted AI suffices and when a governed agentic workflow is required.

| Element         | Required specification                                                            |
|-----------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Unit of analysis | task, tool call, state, workflow step, or automatable decision                    |
| Main signal     | useful autonomy, human interventions, blocked errors, cycle time, traceability    |
| Baseline        | manual process, previous period, comparable group, or current model               |
| Decision        | choose when assisted AI suffices and when governed agentic workflow is needed     |
| Guardrail       | privacy, cost, data quality, interpretability, ownership, operational risk        |
| Main risk       | building agents for tasks needing only better prompts and human ownership         |

Formalization is sound only if a reviewer can reproduce the logic, critique assumptions, and reach the same decision from the same data. If results depend on untracked conversations, the workflow is immature.

## Example or case study

An analytics team moves from free chat to structured workflow: the agent gathers context, generates checks, proposes queries, and requests approval before accessing sensitive data or producing final output.

| Observed evidence                | Cautious interpretation                                   | Recommended action                                |
|--------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Workflow produces faster output | Speed is useful only if quality and control are maintained | Compare with manual baseline and independent review |
| Model offers plausible explanations | Linguistic plausibility is not empirical proof             | Translate each explanation into data or process checks |
| Result improves a metric         | Could be composition effect, leakage, or seasonality       | Segment, verify guardrails, and monitor over time     |

A good case study ends with a defensible decision: what to automate, what to keep manual, which metric to monitor, and which condition would halt the process.

## Operational framework

The workflow unfolds in five steps:

| Step        | Guiding question                              | Minimum output                                  |
|-------------|-----------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Decision    | Which choice should improve?                   | Action verb and owner                           |
| Context     | What do we know about the data?                | Sources, grain, period, limits                  |
| AI support  | Which part can be accelerated?                  | Prompt, tool, or checklist                      |
| Verification | What error could change the conclusion?        | Check, guardrail, baseline                       |
| Handoff     | Who decides and by what criteria?               | Memo, ticket, model, or dashboard                |

This applies to exploratory analysis, SQL, data engineering, AutoML, and agentic workflows. No output should be treated as evidence until linked to source, check, and decision.

## Common mistake to avoid

The first mistake is treating this shift as a label rather than a process, showing outputs without explaining data, checks, and decisions. The second is confusing individual productivity with organizational reliability. The third is failing to decide upfront which actions are allowed, require approval, or are forbidden.

A practical fix is writing three lines before starting:

| Line      | Content                                         |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------|
| Decision  | Which concrete choice do we want to improve?   |
| Evidence  | Which data or check supports the choice?       |
| Stop      | Which risk would stop us or require review?    |

If any are missing, the workflow is not ready. AI can still support exploration but not stable decision-making.

## Lab / exercise

### Basic level

Write a one-page brief for this shift: decision to support, required inputs, expected output, minimal check, and main risk. Use a simple case like a conversion variation or forecast model evaluation.

### Intermediate level

Create a table with three scenarios: manual workflow, AI-assisted workflow, and more automated workflow. For each, indicate estimated time, expected quality, operational risk, necessary check, and final owner.

### Research-grade level

Prepare a reviewable decision memo including hypotheses, required data, exclusion criteria, quality checks, decision threshold, residual risk, and monitoring plan. Add a section "what would change the team's mind."

### Recommended datasets and materials

Use data team process maps, examples of recurring tasks, data access policies, and human intervention logs. If real data is unavailable, create a synthetic dataset with at least 200 rows, a time dimension, a segment dimension, a primary metric, and a field representing a possible quality error.

## Quiz or checkpoint

1. Which concrete decision should this lesson improve?
2. Which part of the workflow can be delegated to AI and which must remain under human ownership?
3. What baseline would you use to measure if the new process truly improves?
4. What failure mode could make an apparently correct output dangerous?
5. Which artifact would you deliver to a stakeholder: memo, dashboard, query review, model card, runbook, or checklist?

If you cannot answer specifically, return to the real problem before continuing. A generic answer often signals premature automation.

## Operational summary

The shift from assisted AI to agentic AI becomes useful when it produces clearer decisions, not just new terminology. Use the cycle decision, context, AI support, verification, and handoff to turn the lesson into verifiable practice.

The final rule is simple: the more the workflow uses tools, state, approvals, and traceability, the more it must be observable. An output you cannot reconstruct, review, or stop is not professional automation; it is just ungoverned speed.
