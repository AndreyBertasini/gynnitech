# CDP and identity resolution

A user who browses on mobile, buys on desktop, and opens emails on a corporate account often appears as three separate profiles. If the CDP under-merges, you waste audience; if it over-merges, you risk privacy breaches, targeting errors, and inflated metrics. The discipline of **CDP and identity resolution** is about striking this operational balance.

## Real problem

In marketing analytics, CDP and identity resolution solve a concrete problem: turning budgets, channels, creatives, and audiences into measurable decisions without confusing volume, attribution, and incrementality. This is not an abstract concept but an operational tool that improves real decisions when applied with reliable data and explicit error thresholds.

## Conceptual model

```mermaid

    A --> B
    B --> C
    C --> D
    D --> E
```

The mental model is sequential: formulate the question, translate it into observable units, assess data quality, then decide. Skipping steps leads to elegant but fragile analyses.

| Step | Guiding question | Expected output |
|---|---|---|
| Framing | What decision needs to change? | A concrete choice, not curiosity |
| Measurement | What signal represents the phenomenon? | Metric, source, and granularity |
| Comparison | Against which baseline do I interpret the result? | Benchmark or plausible counterfactual |
| Action | What do I do if the signal crosses the threshold? | Decision, owner, and next check |

## Rigorous formalization

We formalize CDP and identity resolution as a relation among four elements: unit of analysis, signal, baseline, and decision. The unit can be campaign, cohort, touchpoint, or customer segment; the signal should represent incremental margin, CAC payback, adjusted conversion rate, lift, or retention generated; the baseline is a prior period, holdout group, comparable market, or historical benchmark; the decision is to reallocate resources, change messaging, stop a tactic, or scale an experiment.

| Element | Operational definition |
|---|---|
| Unit | campaign, cohort, touchpoint, or customer segment |
| Signal | incremental margin, CAC payback, adjusted conversion rate, lift, or retention generated |
| Baseline | prior period, holdout group, comparable market, or historical benchmark |
| Decision | reallocate resources, change message, stop tactic, or scale experiment |
| Risk | confusing correlation, data quality, and causal decision-making |

A measure is useful only if it reduces uncertainty about a specific decision. If it does not change a choice, it is documentation; if it changes a choice without controls, it is risk.

## The fragmentation of digital identity: root problem

Every user interaction with a company generates an identifier unique to that channel or device. Without unification, a single user appears as multiple entities: Facebook prospect, anonymous site visitor, email contact, app user, and paying customer. This makes basic questions impossible to answer: "Which Facebook campaign led to the first purchase?" or "How many touchpoints did this customer have before converting?".

**Identity resolution** solves this fragmentation with two methods:

- **Deterministic matching**: links identifiers based on PII such as login or email. Accuracy exceeds 99% and forms the backbone of any reliable identity graph.
- **Probabilistic matching**: uses non-PII signals (IP, device fingerprinting, behavioral patterns) to infer match probabilities. Accuracy varies between 70% and 90% and is increasingly constrained by privacy regulations and GDPR.

Mature systems use deterministic matching to define canonical identity and probabilistic matching only for low-risk enrichment.

## Building the identity graph: architectural approaches

The technical core of a CDP is the **identity graph**, mapping every identifier to a unified customer ID. Two main approaches exist:

**1. SaaS CDP**
- Operation: SDKs collect events and send them to the platform which handles ingestion, validation, identity resolution (black box), and profile creation.
- Advantages: fast setup, user-friendly interfaces, many integrations.
- Disadvantages: vendor lock-in, opaque logic, high MTU-based costs for large anonymous traffic.

**2. Composable CDP (Warehouse-Native)**
- Operation: events collected by open-source tools (Snowplow, Rudderstack) loaded into a data warehouse; SQL transformations with dbt build the graph; Reverse ETL syncs enriched data to operational systems.
- Advantages: full control, transparent and customizable logic, predictable costs, better governance.
- Disadvantages: requires technical skills and longer setup time.

An example SQL logic for an identity graph in dbt illustrates how transparency and testability replace the SaaS black box.

## From graph to unified profile: Zalando case study

Zalando tackles fragmentation with a cloud data lake and warehouse, building an identity graph linking customer_id, hashed email, device_id, and anonymous_id with a reliability hierarchy. Previously siloed data hindered personalization; now the `customer_360` view enables relevant recommendations and complex segments in hours, reducing marketing operational costs by 10-12% and increasing GMV per session.

## Activation and orchestration: Netflix case study

Netflix uses a relatively simple identity graph because the experience is mostly authenticated. Every action is tied to a `member_id` and processed in streaming to update profiles in real time. Their "Reverse ETL" is a continuous process feeding UI microservices with personalized recommendations. This system influences over 80% of watched content, with recommendation click-through lifts exceeding 20% and retention above 90% annually.

## Lab / exercise

### Basic level

Write in five lines a real decision linked to CDP and identity resolution: objective, primary metric, baseline, main risk, and planned action.

### Intermediate level

Build a table with at least three segments or scenarios. For each, indicate signal, possible alternative explanation, and control needed before deciding.

### Research-grade level

Design a validation plan: hypothesis, required data, exclusion criteria, decision threshold, and post-decision control. Specify what would make you change your mind.

### Recommended datasets and materials

Use campaign exports, media costs, web/app events, CRM, transactions, brand surveys, and consent logs. If no real data is available, create a synthetic dataset with 200-500 rows, including a time column, a segment column, an outcome metric, and an exposure variable.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Treating CDP and identity resolution as definitions to memorize rather than a decision protocol. Presenting metrics without baseline, charts without hypotheses, or recommendations without error cost. Always ask: "If this result were false or unstable, what decision would I get wrong?" If unclear, the lesson has not been truly applied.

## Quiz or checkpoint

1. What concrete decision should this lesson improve?
2. Which baseline makes the result interpretable?
3. Which assumption, if wrong, would change the conclusion?
4. What minimum control would you use before presenting the recommendation?

## Operational summary

CDP and identity resolution connect concept, data, and decision. Study them starting from a real problem, formalize the signal, find a credible baseline, build an example, and close with a practical check.
