# Building a Strong Brand: Data and Strategy

Performance marketing drives immediate sales, but demand drops as soon as the budget slows. The real challenge is not choosing between brand and performance, but understanding whether the brand is creating preference, trust, and sustainable pricing. Building a strong brand means turning a vague concept into observable, measurable signals.

## Starting Point

This lesson bridges research, positioning, and finance. A strong brand isn't proven with aspirational slides but defended by showing how it reduces friction, increases choice, or protects margin in competitive markets.

- **Context:** Which positioning do you want to measure?
- **Method:** Which signal distinguishes mere awareness from real preference?
- **Application:** How would you link brand strength to pricing or conversion?

## From Performance to Brand Equity: A Quantitative Shift

The tension between performance marketing and brand building largely stems from differing time horizons and metrics. Performance marketing optimizes short-term acquisition cost; brand building is a long-term investment with subtler but measurable returns.

Many data-driven organizations err by applying the same metrics to both. Expecting a direct ROAS from a brand awareness campaign is like asking a seed to become a tree in a week.

The key concept is **Brand Equity**: the differential value the brand name adds to a product, translating into tangible economic benefits:

1. **Lower CAC over time:** Consumers searching "Nike shoes" cost less to acquire than those searching "running shoes".
2. **Higher willingness-to-pay:** Consumers pay a premium for brands they trust or identify with.
3. **Increased Customer Lifetime Value (LTV):** Strong brand ties boost retention and reduce churn.
4. **Competitive moat:** Years of trust and recognition can't be easily copied.

Measuring progress relies on proxy metrics signaling shifts in perception and behavior. One of the most powerful is **Share of Search**: the share of organic searches for your brand versus total category searches. Les Binet and Peter Field demonstrated a strong correlation between Share of Search and future market share. If your Share of Search exceeds your current market share, it predicts growth.

## Analytical Breakdown of Brand: Four Measurable Pillars

To manage brand measurement, break it into four pillars, each with specific metrics:

1. **Distinctiveness:** The brand must come to mind when needed, via unique assets like colors, sounds, or spokespeople.
   - *Metrics:* Spontaneous and aided brand recall, unique asset recognition, brand search volume.

2. **Relevance:** The brand must be seen as a valid solution to consumer needs.
   - *Metrics:* Consideration rate, category-brand association through textual analysis.

3. **Esteem:** Consumers must trust and respect the brand.
   - *Metrics:* Segmented Net Promoter Score, trust scores, willingness to pay premium.

4. **Knowledge:** Consumers must clearly understand what the brand offers and how it differs.
   - *Metrics:* Value proposition comprehension, awareness and adoption of key features.

These pillars require combining behavioral data with attitudinal insights.

## Quantifying the Competitive Moat: Estimating Brand Premium

Abstract concepts like esteem and trust must be translated into euros. **Conjoint Analysis** is the key technique to isolate the value consumers assign to each product attribute, including brand.

Imagine Bolt, a ride-hailing service, wanting to quantify its brand value against FastRide and Uber. Users see hypothetical rides varying by brand, wait time, price, and driver rating, and choose their preferred option.

Analyzing choices with a multinomial logit model estimates partial utilities for each attribute. The brand premium is calculated as the ratio between the brand coefficient and the price coefficient, converting utility into monetary value.

This figure becomes a strategic KPI: for example, "Consumers are willing to pay €1.25 more for Bolt than FastRide." The goal might be to increase this premium over time, justifying brand-building investments.

## The Brand's Nervous System: Designing a Brand Health Dashboard

You can't run a conjoint analysis weekly. You need agile indicators - a "nervous system" signaling shifts in market perception.

A Brand Health dashboard combines:

- **Leading indicators:** weak, predictive signals like social sentiment, mentions, and share of search.
- **Lagging indicators:** outcome measures like brand recall, NPS, brand premium, and direct traffic.

A good dashboard balances both, enabling early detection and timely response.

### Case Study: Netflix and "Share of Conversation"

Netflix aggregates data from Twitter, Reddit, YouTube, and reviews, using NLP to identify mentions of the brand and key shows. They weight mentions by engagement and compare to total streaming conversations. This metric accurately predicts subscriber growth one quarter ahead.

### Example SQL for Composite Index

A composite brand health index can combine Google Trends, web traffic, and NPS data into a single weighted metric, easing monitoring and leadership communication.

## Practical Lab: From Hypothesis to Metric

### Basic Level

Analyze the Share of Search for "Wise" versus "Revolut" and "Western Union" on Google Trends over five years. Calculate weekly shares and visualize trends, noting any events correlating with spikes or drops.

### Intermediate Level

With an NPS dataset, calculate monthly aggregated and segmented NPS by user cohorts and markets. Check if aggregated NPS masks divergent segment dynamics.

### Advanced Level

Write SQL queries to distinguish "brand-led" from "performance-led" conversions using session and conversion data. Calculate average order value by group and assess profitability.

### Recommended Data and Materials

Use campaign exports, media costs, web/app events, CRM, transactions, brand surveys, and consent logs. If unavailable, create synthetic datasets with temporal, segment, outcome, and exposure variables.

## Common Pitfall to Avoid

Don't treat brand as a memorized concept but as a decision protocol. Presenting metrics without baselines, charts without hypotheses, or recommendations without risk assessment leads to fragile decisions.

A useful check is: "If this result were false, what decision would I get wrong?" If unclear, the lesson hasn't been truly applied.
