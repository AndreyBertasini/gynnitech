# B2B martech operations

In B2B marketing, the core challenge is turning scattered data and signals into reliable operational decisions. Sales teams see promising leads, marketing tracks MQLs, and finance demands a real pipeline: three systems describe the same account with different states. **B2B martech operations** aims to transform this chaos into a measurable process where properties, lifecycle stages, and handoffs do not depend on local interpretations.

## The Core Architecture: The MAP-CRM Dialogue

In B2B, purchase journeys are complex, with long cycles and committee decisions. At the center are two key systems: the Marketing Automation Platform (MAP) and the Customer Relationship Management (CRM).

The MAP (e.g., Marketo, HubSpot) manages Top and Middle of Funnel, tracking digital activities and assigning lead scores based on engagement and fit. The CRM (e.g., Salesforce) manages Accounts, Contacts, and Opportunities, focusing on concrete sales deals.

Data flow between MAP and CRM must be bidirectional and robust:

1. MAP syncs qualified leads (MQLs) to CRM along with engagement activities.
2. CRM updates lead status (e.g., SQL) and sends it back to MAP.

The engineering challenge is ensuring fast synchronization, deduplication, and consistent field mapping across different data models.

## The Account-Based Marketing (ABM) Paradigm: From Individual to Organization

ABM shifts focus from single leads to accounts by identifying high-potential target companies (ICP) and orchestrating personalized campaigns for key decision-makers.

Typical ABM metrics:

- **Account Engagement Score**: aggregates activities of all contacts within a company, weighting role and action.
- **Account Penetration**: percentage of key decision-makers engaged.
- **Account Pipeline Influence**: measures marketing's impact on sales opportunities.

**Case Study: Stripe Atlas**
Stripe Atlas uses ABM focused on startup accelerators as "macro-accounts". Success metrics aggregate at the accelerator level, optimizing resources and outcomes.

## Data Engineering for an "Account 360": Beyond MAP and CRM

A complete view requires integrating data from multiple sources:

- Product usage data (feature adoption, plan limits)
- Support data (tickets, resolution times)
- Financial data (payments, billing)
- Third-party intent data (searches, competitor visits)

The solution is a centralized Data Warehouse (Snowflake, BigQuery) unifying these sources via ETL/ELT and data modeling (dbt).

**Case Study: Gong**
Gong integrates CRM, MAP, product, and intent data in Snowflake, building a `dim_accounts_360` table with aggregated metrics. Using Reverse ETL, this enriched data feeds back into the CRM, giving sellers an up-to-date holistic view without switching tools.

## Calculating an Account Engagement Score in SQL

A concrete ABM model is an aggregated engagement score per account, weighting different marketing activities. The following SQL query demonstrates aggregating site visits, form submissions, and webinar attendance with weighted scores:

```sql
WITH website_visits AS (
    SELECT
        c.account_id,
        SUM(CASE
            WHEN v.session_duration_sec > 300 THEN 5
            WHEN v.session_duration_sec > 60 THEN 2
            ELSE 1
        END) AS visit_score
    FROM raw_data.web_visits v
    JOIN raw_data.contacts c ON v.user_email = c.email
    WHERE v.visit_timestamp >= CURRENT_DATE - 90
    GROUP BY c.account_id
),
form_fills AS (
    SELECT
        c.account_id,
        SUM(CASE
            WHEN f.form_name = 'request_demo' THEN 50
            WHEN f.form_name = 'contact_sales' THEN 40
            WHEN f.form_name = 'ebook_download' THEN 10
            ELSE 5
        END) AS form_score
    FROM raw_data.form_submissions f
    JOIN raw_data.contacts c ON f.user_email = c.email
    WHERE f.submission_timestamp >= CURRENT_DATE - 90
    GROUP BY c.account_id
),
webinar_attendance AS (
    SELECT
        c.account_id,
        SUM(CASE
            WHEN w.attended_percentage > 0.75 THEN 30
            WHEN w.attended_percentage > 0.25 THEN 15
            ELSE 5
        END) AS webinar_score
    FROM raw_data.webinar_logs w
    JOIN raw_data.contacts c ON w.user_email = c.email
    WHERE w.webinar_date >= CURRENT_DATE - 90
    GROUP BY c.account_id
)
SELECT
    acc.account_id,
    acc.account_name,
    acc.industry,
    COALESCE(wv.visit_score, 0) AS visit_score,
    COALESCE(ff.form_score, 0) AS form_score,
    COALESCE(wa.webinar_score, 0) AS webinar_score,
    (COALESCE(wv.visit_score, 0) + COALESCE(ff.form_score, 0) + COALESCE(wa.webinar_score, 0)) AS total_engagement_score
FROM raw_data.accounts acc
LEFT JOIN website_visits wv ON acc.account_id = wv.account_id
LEFT JOIN form_fills ff ON acc.account_id = ff.account_id
LEFT JOIN webinar_attendance wa ON acc.account_id = wa.account_id
WHERE
    (wv.visit_score IS NOT NULL OR ff.form_score IS NOT NULL OR wa.webinar_score IS NOT NULL)
ORDER BY total_engagement_score DESC
LIMIT 100;
```

This model helps sales prioritize outreach to the most engaged accounts, boosting efficiency and response rates.

## Hands-On: Martech Data Engineering Lab

### Basic Level

Write a five-line summary of a real decision linked to B2B martech operations: objective, primary metric, baseline, main risk, and planned action.

### Intermediate Level

Build a table with three segments or scenarios. For each, specify the signal, alternative explanation, and control needed before deciding.

### Research-Grade Level

Design a validation plan: hypotheses, required data, exclusion criteria, decision threshold, and post-decision control. Specify what would make you change your mind.

### Recommended Datasets and Materials

Use campaign exports, media costs, web/app events, CRM, transactions, brand surveys, and consent logs. If real data is unavailable, create a synthetic dataset with at least a time column, segment, outcome metric, and exposure variable.

## Common Pitfall to Avoid

Treating B2B martech operations as a memorized definition instead of a decision protocol. Presenting metrics without baselines, charts without hypotheses, or recommendations without error cost leads to fragile decisions.

Always ask: "If this result were false or unstable, what wrong decision would I make?" If unclear, the lesson has not been truly applied.

## Quiz or Checkpoint

1. What concrete decision should this lesson improve?
2. What baseline makes the result interpretable?
3. What assumption, if wrong, would change the conclusion?
4. What minimal control would you use before presenting the recommendation?

## Operational Summary

B2B martech operations require linking concept, data, and decision. Start from a real problem, formalize the signal, find a credible baseline, build an example, and close with a practical control. Only then does knowledge become operational skill.
