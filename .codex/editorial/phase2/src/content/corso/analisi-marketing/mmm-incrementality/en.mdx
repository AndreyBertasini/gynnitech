## Real-world problem

Marketing leaders often want to know if investments in TV, paid search, or promotions actually generated incremental sales. Yet, tracking individual users is impossible, and markets shift with seasonality, pricing, and competitor actions. Marketing Mix Modeling (MMM) addresses this complexity by providing a rigorous, aggregated view when individual attribution falls short.

## Conceptual model

MMM represents a paradigm shift: it does not analyze individual user journeys but measures marketing investments' impact at an aggregate level, such as a region or country over time. The key question is counterfactual: what would sales have been without investment in a specific channel? The answer comes from comparing observed sales to this hypothetical scenario.

Each step is essential: framing the question, translating it into observable data, assessing data quality, and making decisions based on results. Skipping any step leads to fragile conclusions.

| Step | Guiding question | Expected output |
|---|---|---|
| Framing | What decision needs to change? | A concrete choice, not curiosity |
| Measurement | What signal represents the phenomenon? | Metric, source, granularity |
| Comparison | Against which baseline do I interpret results? | Benchmark or plausible counterfactual |
| Action | What do I do if signal exceeds threshold? | Decision, owner, next check |

## Rigorous formalization

MMM formalizes the relation between unit of analysis, signal, baseline, and decision:

| Element | Operational definition |
|---|---|
| Unit | Campaign, cohort, touchpoint, or customer segment |
| Signal | Incremental margin, CAC payback, lift, retention generated |
| Baseline | Previous period, holdout group, comparable market |
| Decision | Shift resources, change message, stop tactic, scale experiment |
| Risk | Confusing correlation with causal decision-making |

A measure is useful only if it reduces uncertainty about a specific decision.

## Example or case study

A growth team must decide whether to increase budget on a channel with low CPA but weak marginal sales. Using MMM, they create a three-column reading: what we know, what we assume, and what decision we take.

| Evidence | Prudent interpretation | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Positive but unisolated signal | Phenomenon exists, cause uncertain | Seek baseline or holdout |
| Segment with different response | Average effect hides heterogeneity | Analyze cohorts or subgroups |
| Rising operational cost | Evaluate on margin | Apply economic thresholds |

## Model mechanics

MMM is a multivariate regression decomposing sales into components:

Sales_t = β₀ (Baseline) + Σ(β_i · f(ChannelSpend_it)) + Σ(γ_j · Control_jt) + ε_t

- β₀ is the baseline sales without marketing spend.
- Σ(β_i · f(ChannelSpend_it)) is channel contribution; β_i measures efficiency, f() models adstock and saturation.
- Σ(γ_j · Control_jt) are control variables (seasonality, promotions, competitors, macroeconomics).
- ε_t is the model error.

## Temporal dynamics and saturation

Marketing impact is neither immediate nor linear. Adstock captures advertising effect persistence:

Adstock_t = Spend_t + λ · Adstock_{t-1}

λ is the decay rate (0-1). Channels like TV have high λ, Search lower.

Saturation reflects diminishing returns, modeled by the Hill function:

Contribution(Spend) = β · Spend^α / (γ^α + Spend^α)

- β is maximum contribution.
- α controls curve steepness.
- γ is half-saturation point.

## From lab to strategy

Two emblematic cases:

- Airbnb used MMM to find SEM's marginal ROI nearly zero in mature markets, reallocating budget to brand marketing with strong carry-over effects.
- Revolut implemented a Bayesian MMM to measure paid influencer impact, revealing a 12% increase in organic acquisitions.

## Lab / exercise

### Basic level

Write five lines: objective, primary metric, baseline, main risk, and planned action for a real MMM-related decision.

### Intermediate level

Create a table with at least three segments or scenarios, indicating signal, possible alternative explanation, and needed control.

### Research-grade level

Design a validation plan with hypotheses, required data, exclusion criteria, decision threshold, and post-decision check.

### Recommended datasets and materials

Use campaign exports, media costs, web/app events, CRM, transactions, brand surveys, and consent logs. If unavailable, create a synthetic dataset with at least one temporal column, one segment column, one outcome metric, and one exposure variable.

## Common pitfall to avoid

Treating MMM as a memorized definition rather than a decision-making protocol. Presenting metrics without baseline, charts without hypotheses, or recommendations without error cost leads to risky choices.

Always ask: "If this result were false, what wrong decision would I make?" If you cannot answer, you have not truly applied the lesson.

## Quiz or checkpoint

1. What concrete decision should this lesson improve?
2. Which baseline makes the result interpretable?
3. Which assumption, if wrong, would change the conclusion?
4. What minimal control would you use before presenting the recommendation?

## Operational summary

Marketing Mix Modeling is a discipline turning complex data into strategic decisions under uncertainty. It requires clearly linking problem, data, and action, avoiding confusion between correlation and causation. It is an advanced but essential tool to allocate budgets effectively and measure the true incrementality of marketing activities.
