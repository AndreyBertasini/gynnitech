In 2019, Zalando ran an experiment that challenged many CFOs' assumptions. They stopped showing retargeting ads to a group of loyal customers for six weeks and found conversions dropped by only 2%. This revealed that these customers would have bought anyway, regardless of the ads. Yet platform reports showed an apparent ROAS over 8x - a mirage. Ninety-eight percent of that revenue was baseline, not incremental.

## Why your ROAS dashboard can deceive you

Standard attribution models like last-click assign full credit to the last touchpoint before purchase, ignoring whether the campaign actually caused the sale or was merely present along the path. This inflates the perceived effectiveness of retargeting and brand search campaigns.

| Attribution Model | How it works | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Last-click | 100% credit to last touchpoint | Confuses correlation with causation, favors retargeting |
| First-click | 100% credit to first touchpoint | Ignores nurturing and decision stages |
| Linear | Equal credit to all touchpoints | Assumes equal weight, rarely true |
| Data-driven (Google) | ML distributes credit based on patterns | Measures correlation, not causality |

None answer the key question: "If this campaign hadn't existed, would these sales have happened anyway?" For that, you need an experimental approach.

## Incrementality: definition and formula

Incrementality measures the causal effect of a campaign:

**Incremental Sales = Sales in exposed group - Sales in control group**

The control group is identical except it did not receive the campaign. The operational metric is iROAS (incremental Return on Ad Spend):

$$iROAS = \frac{\text{Incremental Revenue}}{\text{Ad Spend}}$$

If platform ROAS is 6x but iROAS is 1.8x, only 30% of attributed revenue is truly caused.

## Three methods to measure real incrementality

### 1. Geo-Lift Test

Split the market into two comparable geographic areas: one exposed to the campaign, one not. Compare sales growth to estimate causal effect.

**Example:** TV campaign in Lombardy (test) vs Piedmont (control). After 4 weeks, Lombardy sales up 14%, Piedmont 6%, so estimated incrementality is 8%.

The included Python code demonstrates difference-in-differences analysis, normalizing series and calculating iROAS.

**Limitations:** Choosing regions with parallel historical trends is critical; local events can bias results.

### 2. Conversion Lift Study (Meta and Google)

Platforms randomly split your audience into test and control groups. Test group sees ads; control does not. Comparing conversions estimates incrementality.

Advantages: user-level randomization, gold standard for causality.

Disadvantages: conflict of interest, often optimistic results, more reliable for prospecting than retargeting.

### 3. Propensity Score Analysis (PSA)

Uses machine learning to estimate each user's probability of exposure. Compares outcomes of similar users exposed and not exposed to estimate effect.

Advantages: works with observational data, useful when randomization isn't possible.

Disadvantages: requires all relevant variables; hidden confounders bias estimates.

## Calculating and interpreting iROAS

A simplified example shows a platform ROAS of 6x translating to a real iROAS of 1.4x, with 77% of attributed revenue baseline. This drastically changes how effectiveness is perceived and guides budget reallocation.

## Expected incrementality by channel

| Channel | Typical Incrementality | Reasoning | Platform ROAS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand Search | 10-30% | Users already intent-driven, organic converts anyway | 15-30x |
| Retargeting | 20-50% | High contamination from users ready to buy | 4-8x |
| Social Prospecting | 50-75% | Genuine new demand created | 2-4x |
| Influencer & Awareness | 60-85% | Creates demand from scratch | 1-3x |
| Email to existing customers | 30-60% | Varies by campaign type | 3-15x |

### The brand search surprise

Many believe brand search with ROAS 25x is pure gold. In reality, removing the ad mostly shifts clicks to organic results. eBay showed incrementality often below 5%. Reallocating budget from brand search to prospecting can improve outcomes.

## Incrementality and statistical significance

Reliable tests require adequate samples: at least 50 regions or 4-6 weeks for geo-lift, 10,000 conversions for conversion lift, 1,000 exposed and 1,000 unexposed users for PSA.

## Case study: Italian retailer finds 40% of Meta budget non-incremental

A fashion retailer found 41% of Meta budget spent on segments with iROAS near 1x - no incremental sales. By shifting budget to new users and look-alike audiences, revenue stayed flat or slightly positive, acquisition cost dropped 28%, and lifetime value increased.

## Building an incrementality culture

The biggest barrier is cultural: easy KPIs like ROAS seem more impressive. To change this:

- Start with a critical channel and rigorous tests
- Frame budget reallocation as optimization, not criticism
- Integrate iROAS into regular dashboards

## Incrementality and HADI Cycles

Incrementality testing is an advanced part of the HADI framework for systematically testing marketing hypotheses.

## Conclusion

Measuring only ROAS without incrementality is like evaluating a drug without a control group - you can't tell what caused the effect. Only by knowing iROAS can you pay for what you truly caused, avoiding wasted budget on sales that would have happened anyway.

Top marketers go beyond ROAS to understand iROAS. This is the discipline of making decisions under uncertainty, distinguishing correlation from causation.
