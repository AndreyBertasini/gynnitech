In 2019, Patagonia stopped advertising on Facebook and Instagram not just for ethics but because their data showed it cost four times more to reach their actual target on those platforms compared to niche channels like mountaineering magazines or outdoor podcasts. This decision was driven by calculating the Affinity Index, which measures how much a population segment is over- or under-represented in a media audience compared to the general population.

## Real-world problem

Many media plans confuse cost per thousand impressions (CPM) with cost per relevant impression. This leads to wasted budgets on audiences unlikely to buy, and can even harm brand perception.

## Conceptual model

Affinity Index is calculated as:

Affinity Index = (percentage of target in media audience / percentage of target in total population) × 100

An index of 100 means the segment behaves like the average population; above 100 means over-represented; below 100 means under-represented.

## Rigorous formalization

| Media | % Target in Audience | % Target in Population | Affinity Index | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mountaineering Magazine | 38% | 6% | 633 | 6 times concentration |
| "Trail Talk" Podcast | 29% | 6% | 483 | Nearly 5 times concentration |
| Generic Instagram | 7% | 6% | 117 | Close to average |
| Generic TikTok | 3% | 6% | 50 | Half the target concentration |

These figures show that each impression on the specialized magazine is six times more likely to reach the target than on generic Instagram.

## Example or case study

For a premium outdoor brand with a 6% online population target, nominal CPM alone is insufficient. The in-target CPM is calculated by dividing nominal CPM by (Affinity Index / 100). Thus, a low CPM on a media with Affinity Index 50 can be less efficient than a higher CPM on media with Affinity Index 300.

| Media | CPM | Affinity Index | In-Target CPM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generic TikTok | €2 | 50 | €4.00 |
| Programmatic Display | €2.5 | 70 | €3.57 |
| Generic Instagram | €5 | 117 | €4.27 |
| Mountaineering Magazine | €60 | 633 | €9.48 |

## Lab / exercise

### Basic level

Calculate the Affinity Index for two media using public or simulated data by comparing the target percentage in the audience versus the population.

### Intermediate level

Use a customer database to segment the target and calculate Affinity Index for product categories or interests, as shown in the SQL example.

### Research-grade level

Integrate CRM data with customer match on Meta or Google platforms to obtain Affinity Index based on actual customers rather than demographic proxies.

### Recommended datasets and materials

Anonymized CRM datasets, Meta Audience Insights reports, online panel surveys.

## Common mistake to avoid

Planning media mix based solely on nominal CPM without considering Affinity Index leads to wasted budget on irrelevant audiences.

## Conclusion

Affinity Index turns media planning decisions from guesses into data-driven choices. Patagonia's example shows that knowing the true concentration of your target in each media allows efficient budget allocation and reduces waste. Embedding this metric into workflows and monitoring it over time is key to adapting to audience shifts and maximizing campaign impact.
