In 2004 Lego faced a deep crisis: sales down 35% in three years and a loss of $300 million. A traditional market research of half a million dollars concluded that children no longer had patience for bricks, leading the company to launch simplified sets, which however made the situation worse.

In 2005 Henning Beck changed his approach: he watched the children play in their natural environment in ten different countries, discovering unexpected details like a German child who consumed the soles of skate shoes for social status. Lego responded with the Bionicle line, complex and with a deep narrative, which became a lasting success.

This story shows the limit of traditional research: they measure what people say, not what they do or feel, and they arrive too late to influence decisions.

## Why traditional research fails

Traditional market research is slow and expensive, designed for large companies in the 1950s. They often produce long reports that end up in a drawer, without changing concrete decisions.

| Size | Traditional Research | Agile Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 3-6 months | 1-2 weeks |
| Cost | 20,000-100,000 euros | EUR 500-5,000 |
| Output | 200-page report | 5 slides with operable insights |
| Who reads it? | Analysts | Decision-making team |
| Real use | Rarely | Change decisions within 30 days |
| Insight type | Description | Activable |

Henry Ford sensed that customers express needs in terms of existing solutions, not possible solutions, and that's why a different approach is needed.

## The 7 rock 'n' roll methods

### 1. the "Long coffee" (Blue interview)

Five 30-minute interviews allow you to identify 85% of usability problems, thanks to individual context and in-depth conversation. Key questions are:

1. How likely is it that you recommend us? (NPS instant) 2. What do you prefer about the product? 3. If you could change one thing, what would it be?

The third question generates specific operational insights, often ignored in official channels.

**Cost:** 15 euros. **Time:**One afternoon. **Impact:**Insight structural insight.

### 2. the "Fake landing page" (Smoke test)

Before developing a product, you create a landing page that explAIns the idea and invites you to subscribe to a beta or pre-order. Real behavior (click, register) measures concrete interest.

| Variant | When to use it | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-order page | Physical product | €300-500 |
| WAItlist | SaaS, app | €100-300 |
| Demo Booking | B2B services | €0-100 |
| CampAIgn ads | Any | €300-1.000 |
| Content Offer | Lead gen | €0-200 |

**Time:**One week. **Impact:**RAPId validation before investing months.

### 3. the "Warman poll" (5 questions, 3 minutes)

Short surveys increase the completion rate. An effective template for the B2B includes open and multiple choice questions to understand problems, used tools, expense, satisfaction and desires.

**Cost:** 0-50 euros. **Time:** 2-3 days.

### 4. the "Reddit research" (Social listening structured)

Analyzing reviews, forums and social media allows you to discover pAIn points, used language and real alternatives at no extra cost.

In Italy, useful resources are subreddit such as r/italy, specialized forums and reviews on Trustpilot or Google.

### 5. the "Competitor safari" (Mystery shopping)

Buying and using competing products for a week allows you to identify strengths and weaknesses, especially in post-purchase.

Checklist includes customer acquisition, ease of purchase, onboarding, follow-up and customer service.

**Cost:** Product price. **Time:** 1-2 weeks.

### 6. the "Data diving" (Analysis of existing data)

Before collecting new data, analyze internal data: Google Analytics, CRM, emAIl marketing, customer service, return data, recorded sessions.

A concrete example: a clothing company reduced returns by 22% in a quarter thanks to the analysis of the reasons for return.

**Cost:** 0 euros. **Time:** 2-3 days.

### 7. the "Micro-Experiment" (Test on the field)

Testing directly on the market, for example by introducing a delivery option in a limited area or a new packaging on a reference, allows you to measure real behavior.

**Time:** 2-4 weeks. **Impact:**Concrete behavioral data.

## The framework "1-1-1": triangulated search

Three types of evidence are needed for strategic decisions:

- 1 quantitative figure (e.g. A/B test), 1 qualitative figure (e.g. interviews), 1 direct observation (e.g. mystery shopping)

Only with all three you can act with high confidence.

## How to present the results

An effective report is limited to five slides:

1. The question you answer 2. The most important number 3. Three key insights with direct quotes 4. What you change right away 5. How to measure success

The slide of the insights must contain quotes that explAIn the context, not just numerical data.

## Case study: music industry, from gut feel to data-driven

An Italian label reduced the rate of bankruptcy of contracts from 70% to 35% using micro-experiments on TikTok, smoke test, data diving and flash interviews. The selection of artists became more precise without increasing the budget.

## Join agile research and data science

It is not a question of choosing between agile and rigorous, but of integrating them: interviews and smoke tests to define problems and hypotheses, micro-experiments and A/B tests to experiment, triangulation and predictive models to validate and scale.
